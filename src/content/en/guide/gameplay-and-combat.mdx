Crimson Moon's gameplay loop is: **equip a loadout at your hub, dive through a portal into fallen Gildenarch, clear zones, stack boons, purify your loot, and return to upgrade before the next run.** Combat is fast, gory melee — you tear through demons, undead, vampires, and Hellgrowth monstrosities with visceral, stylish executions.

## The Run Loop

Each run sends your Nephilim into the Wards of Gildenarch. As you clear each zone, you collect **boons** — benefits that can add more spells or more charge for your Nephilim mode. Zone clears also automatically refresh your healing supplies, so momentum between zones matters. The run ends when you die or finish your objective, and the roguelike part kicks in: what you earned carries into permanent upgrades.

## Purify Loot and the Blacksmith

Loot you grab during a run is not usable as-is — **Purify Loot** is how you process it, and the **blacksmith in the hub** handles your upgrade system between runs. Launch-week guides keep hammering the same advice: learn the purify loop early. One popular beginner video ("Best Start, Purify Loot And Boon Stacking") built its whole route around it, and a previewer noted the upgrade system via the hub blacksmith was already in during the demo.

## Nephilim Mode, Shields, and Healing

Your Nephilim mode is a transformation with its own charge meter — boons can add spells or more charge for it, and it functions a bit like swapping into a more powerful class mid-fight. A **shield** mechanic layers on top of your health, and healing comes from **three healing potions**: you collect them from the dead bodies of fallen Nephilim soldiers across the map, and zone clears top you back up automatically.

## Builds Are Real — Ask the "Infinite Loop" Guy

Builds matter enough that the launch-week meta is already forming. Players have found genuinely broken combinations — one streamer cleared content with an **infinite loop build in 20 minutes** and called it "broke the game" territory, while others push build-first approaches before you even touch the harder Wards. Expect the meta to shift with patches; the studio shipped Steam patch 1.0.0.1 on September 3, 2026, three days after launch.

## Fighting Solo or With a Friend

Everything above works solo or in **2-player online co-op**. The top of the food chain — the **Savage Dead Gods** — is where brutal boss fights live, and that is where the soulslike-leaning combat shows its teeth.

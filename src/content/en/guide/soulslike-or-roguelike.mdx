Short answer: **Crimson Moon is both, partially.** The official Steam tags list it as an Action, Adventure, and RPG — but the game's actual structure blends **soulslike-leaning melee combat** with a **roguelike run loop**. Reviewer outlet The Outerhaven put it best in its September 1 review: Crimson Moon is "a soulslike and a roguelike had a baby, and it's pretty great."

## The Soulslike Side

Combat is deliberate, melee-first, and punishing. You tear through demons, undead, vampires, and Hellgrowth monstrosities with visceral, bloody executions, and official material openly advertises "brutal bosses." If your reference point is the genre's core — timing, commitment to swings, and boss fights that end runs — Crimson Moon speaks that language.

## The Roguelike Side

The structure is where it clearly parts ways with a pure soulslike. Crimson Moon is organized around **runs** through the fallen city of Gildenarch: you equip a loadout, dive through a portal, clear zones, and collect **boons** that add benefits like extra spells or more charge for your Nephilim mode. Between runs, an upgrade system via the **blacksmith in the hub** carries your progress forward, and **Purify Loot** is part of managing what you bring home. One launch-week player even broke the game with an "infinite loop" build in 20 minutes — very roguelike behavior.

## So Which Genre Is It?

The honest label is a **gothic action-adventure RPG with soulslike and roguelike elements**. It is not a pure soulslike (the run structure and boons see to that), and it is not a pure roguelike (the deliberate melee and boss design lean soulslike).
